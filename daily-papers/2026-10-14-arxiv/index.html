    Euler: Towards Human-Centered Multi-Agent Mathematical Discovery Qiuyu Chen 2026-10-14 PDF Mathematical research drives scientific discovery, and recent advances in AI for mathematics have shown that language-model agents can solve challenging problems and develop substantial mathematical proofs. Yet much of today's AI-for-mathematics work centers on obtaining a final answer. This emphasis leaves the exploration behind a result difficult for researchers to understand, while long-running investigations face fragmented context and the risk of unsupported claims propagating across reasoning steps. We present Euler, a human-centered mathematical research system built around a shared, structured representation of the research process. Euler organizes mathematical statements, assumptions, proof dependencies, alternative approaches, failed attempts, and verification evidence into connected research graphs. This representation allows researchers to trace how results are developed and supplies agents with persistent, task-relevant knowledge for planning, collaboration, and verification across sessions. Researchers can also select a graph node linked to a saved team state, revisit the agents' earlier reasoning, and explore alternative directions through continued interaction. We illustrate Euler's capabilities through three mathematical research case studies and describe the implemented system and its software validation.
    When Extremists Enter LLM Societies: Investigating Extreme Behavior in Multi-Agent Systems Guo Chen 2026-10-14 PDF Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly serve as computational actors in multi-agent social simulations. While existing work emphasizes representative personas and average-case behavior, social science shows that persistent tail actors can disproportionately shape collective dynamics. We study this regularity in controlled LLM-based multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS). In these systems, such actors cannot be reduced to fixed scalar states: prompts, natural-language histories, and backbone generation mediate their behavior and influence. We first analyzed three properties of extremists in sociology and model the LLM extremist based on LLM-native perspective. We introduce EXAM, an EXtremist Agent Modeling framework that capable of configuring extremist agents for specific tasks and precisely controlling their population proportion, spatial distribution, interaction protocols, and measurement interfaces. Across four scenarios: congressional election, financial crisis, epidemic public opinion, and iterated prisoner's dilemma, we observe that small persistent tail groups produce measurable, backbone- and scenario-dependent collective shifts. EXAM shows how this social-science regularity manifests through language-mediated mechanisms specific to LLM-based social simulation.
    On the Optimisation Landscape of Overparameterised Weighted MMD Tharrshann Jayan Logarajah 2026-10-14 PDF We study the optimisation landscape of the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) over weighted atomic measures. For the one-dimensional Gaussian kernel, we show that, for any compactly supported target distribution, sufficiently many particles ensure that every local minimum has arbitrarily small MMD. Moreover, any stationary point whose MMD remains bounded away from zero is unstable under an unweighted gradient flow over the particle locations. These results hold both when the weights are constrained to the simplex, and when they are unconstrained but optimised at each step. When the target is itself finitely supported, we further strengthen this result: beyond an explicit particle threshold, there are no spurious local minima, and every spurious stationary point is unstable. To our knowledge, these are the first finite-particle overparameterisation guarantees of their kind for weighted MMD optimisation.
    Text-Informed Training Data Filtering for Enhanced Virtual Flow Meter Performance Kristoffer Hjelmen Nesland 2026-10-14 PDF In sectors such as energy, aquaculture and chemical processing, the effectiveness of both mechanistic and data-driven models is frequently limited by poor-quality training data, which obscures underlying physical laws and degrades predictive accuracy. Such quality issues commonly manifest as anomalies in the dataset, ranging from sensor faults to process upsets and incorrect operator inputs. While traditional rule-based filtering can remove a subset of the anomalies, it is unable to leverage contextual information stored as unstructured text, such as engineer comments and operational logs that explain under what conditions the data was collected. We propose a text-informed data filtering method that leverages large language models (LLMs) to integrate unstructured operational data with tabular sensor data. The method consists of two steps: (1) contextual anomaly detection by reasoning over both data modalities, and (2) generation of a binary mask that is applied to filter the training dataset for model development. To investigate the potential benefit, a steady-state soft-sensor regression model, namely a virtual flow meter that estimates well flow rates, is used as the downstream evaluation target. Experiments on a synthetic oil and gas dataset show improved model performance compared to traditional rule-based filtering. Ablation studies show that textual operator comments can provide filtering-relevant signals beyond sensor data alone, with the multimodal approach reducing downstream MAPE from 7.1% to 5.4% relative to sensor-only filtering. This work demonstrates that integrating unstructured operational knowledge with sensor data through LLMs can improve data quality for industrial machine learning applications, offering a scalable alternative to manual data curation.
    OmniPoser: Flexible Human Motion Recovery in the Wild with Masked Flow Matching Minghao Liu 2026-10-14 PDF Recovering 3D human motion from sparse wearable sensors is challenging due to the inherent noise, occlusion, and instability of real-world tracking signals. Existing approaches are restricted to fixed sensor configurations and, when built on diffusion models, are computationally prohibitive for real-time deployment. We propose OmniPoser, a universal framework that recovers full-body SMPL motion from a flexible combination of heterogeneous inputs, such as 3D keypoints, sparse IMU rotations, head-mounted 6-DoF poses, or any subset thereof, within a single model. OmniPoser introduces a cross-modal masking mechanism paired with a Masked Conditioning Encoder, enabling one architecture to incorporate diverse sensor configurations through a masked design. A decoupled dual-stream generative backbone generates complete motion via Conditional Flow Matching, requiring only a single ODE integration step at inference. Across three settings and seven benchmarks, including in-the-wild Nymeria and Ego-Exo4D datasets, OmniPoser performs on par with state-of-the-art methods while running at over 700 FPS. The project page is available at https://jamesbridgewater51.github.io/omniposer-eccv/.
    Lost in the Fallback: How Reliably Do Typed Decision Models Use Fallback Options? Yuanbo Xie 2026-10-14 PDF Typed decision models (TDMs) are increasingly used in applications such as request routing and model selection, making reliable decisions critical to system performance. Fallback options accommodate categories outside regular choices, enabling deferral to human experts or more capable models. While recent works have examined adversarial vulnerabilities in TDMs, their ability to follow fallback rules in non-adversarial settings remains underexplored. We introduce the Paired Fallback Robustness (PFR) Test to evaluate two aspects of fallback reliability: whether models correctly switch between regular and fallback options, and whether they make consistent decisions under logically equivalent definitions of the same rule. We evaluate open-source decision models on three benchmarks, with controlled comparisons across architectures and parameter counts. We find that correct recognition does not reliably transfer to correct fallback options, and errors persist even when the correct category is explicitly supplied.Moreover, logically equivalent formulations of the same fallback rule can also produce substantial disagreement in model decisions. These findings reveal a crucial reliability gap in current TDMs: fallback mechanisms designed to improve reliability can instead introduce errors. It highlights faithful execution of fallback rules and consistency under equivalent definitions as essential requirements for reliable typed decision-making.
    Energy Consumption Analysis of Discrete Diffusion and Autoregressive Language Models Boris Ruf 2026-10-14 PDF As Large Language Models (LLMs) continue to scale, their energy consumption during inference has become a critical concern. While Autoregressive (AR) models are the industry standard, Diffusion Language Models (DLLMs) have emerged as a promising alternative capable of parallel token generation. In this paper, we perform a comparative energy benchmark of diffusion models (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) against AR counterparts (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B). We analyze the trade-offs between model architecture, KV-cache optimization, and Joules-per-token efficiency. Our results show that at longer sequences (200--600 tokens), diffusion models achieve 2.5--2.7 J/token, approximately 2$\times$ more efficient than AR models with KV-cache (5.3--5.7 J/token). KV-cache provides substantial benefits for AR models at longer sequences (50--70\% energy reduction).
    DeltaStreamer: Towards Human-Like Agentic Streaming Video Understanding Boyuan Sun 2026-10-14 PDF We propose DeltaStreamer, a novel agentic framework for streaming video understanding inspired by how humans observe, remember, and investigate. Streaming video understanding requires a system to retain evidence before knowing which details future queries will need. Existing approaches often obscure event transitions and omit relevant details when compressing video history, and cannot recover missing visual evidence. DeltaStreamer addresses these limitations by combining change-aware memory with query-driven planning and targeted evidence gathering with replay. Before a query arrives, it continuously observes the stream and explicitly records visual changes in structured memory, preserving how events unfold rather than only what is present. When a query arrives, a query-driven planner identifies the required evidence and guides memory retrieval and targeted replay. By revisiting historical frames, the agent can recover visual details omitted during observation and ground its answer in evidence beyond the retained descriptions. This connects continuous observation with active investigation tailored to each question. Experiments on OVO-Bench and StreamingBench demonstrate improvements over representative streaming video understanding methods, with overall scores of 66.07% and 59.53%, respectively. Project page: https://bbbbchan.github.io/DeltaStreamer/
    OpenTWBench (Open Taiwan Benchmark): Taiwan-Domain Knowledge Evaluation for Large Language Models, and How the Measurement Apparatus Shapes the Score Liang-Hsun Huang 2026-10-14 PDF We present OpenTWBench, an open evaluation suite for Taiwan-domain knowledge in large language models, built around two benchmarks. tw-exam-bench contains 255,854 four-option questions across 148 academic subjects, from fifteen years of Taiwan's national examinations published as open data by the Ministry of Examination; answer keys agree across 31,312 duplicated question groups at 99.91%, with all 27 disagreements accounted for. A reviewed second pass withdrew 2,180 items whose stems depend on a figure the text does not carry; all scores use the remaining 253,674. tw-formosa-bench contains 1,696 difficulty-curated questions about everyday and contemporary Taiwan - the Taiwan that licensing examinations never ask about - generated from a community-curated corpus under a discipline in which no model writes a ground truth, and curated against a frozen seven-model pool so that the pool scored at the passing margin (46.9-74.6%, median ~54%); a leave-one-out experiment shows the selection preserves model ranking (near-zero bias). Within six weeks the frontier had cleared that band: of 108 models now on the public board, 22 score above its ceiling, the best 91.6%. We also document how much of a score comes from the apparatus rather than the model. On identical questions, switching answer extraction from pattern matching to an explicit \boxed{} answer moves accuracy from 20.8% to 93.9%; a single placeholder character in a system prompt moved one model from last (58.2%) to first (83.9%) on the same benchmark; doubling the thinking budget from 8k to 16k tokens bought 0.2% while shuffling which questions failed; and a three-judge validation panel proved to be one judge in disguise, two members approving 92-99% of everything shown. We release all datasets, per-item difficulty data, curation manifests and a leaderboard at https://opentwbench.ai
    VisibilitySplat: Ray-Traced Occlusion for Relighting with 3D Gaussian Splatting Sven Kluge 2026-10-14 PDF We address inverse rendering with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing explicit visibility modeling for relighting as well as material and light decomposition. Our approach augments the standard rasterized 3DGS pipeline with a sparse visibility oracle that estimates transmittance-weighted occlusion for Gaussian primitives from sampled lighting directions. This oracle is implemented using a point-based ray tracer accelerated by a bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) and queried sparsely within the inverse-rendering objectives, while the original 3DGS loop is retained for training efficiency, interactive visualization, and tooling compatibility. Shading follows a physically based rendering (PBR) formulation with a principled microfacet bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF) and low-resolution high-dynamic-range (HDR) environment maps. We evaluate our method on the TensoIR synthetic dataset against baselines based on neural radiance fields (NeRFs), tensor representations, and 3DGS. The results show competitive performance and improvements over several earlier baselines for novel-view synthesis (NVS), albedo reconstruction, and relighting. Ablations indicate that explicit visibility modeling is an important contributor to relighting and material-illumination disentanglement, while pure PBR supervision can favor appearance fitting in NVS. Overall, the findings suggest that sparse, point-based visibility queries effectively complement rasterized splatting for inverse rendering while preserving the practical advantages of the 3DGS ecosystem.
