| An mHealth intervention based on behavior change techniques to promote physical activity and nutrition in older patients with cancer: protocol for an N-of-1 trial | Brusseau, M. | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.06.26356658v1.full.pdf) | BACKGROUND: Older adults with cancer often struggle to achieve recommended levels of physical activity and dietary intake. Ecological momentary assessment combined with accelerometry can provide insights into the temporal dynamics of psychological and behavioral processes at the individual level, such as motivation towards health behaviors. OBJECTIVE: This N-of-1 study aims to improve physical activity and dietary intake among older patients with cancer using an mHealth behavioral intervention. METHODS: A single-subject ABA' design will be employed among older patients with cancer ([&ge;] 70 years). A 2-week baseline phase (A) will be followed by an 8-week intervention phase (B) and a 2-week follow-up phase (A'). Throughout all these phases, participants will complete a daily data collection protocol combining ecological momentary assessment questionnaires and an ActiGraph wGT3X-BT accelerometer worn on the waist to measure physical activity. Ecological momentary assessment questionnaires will be delivered via a digital application to collect information on dietary intake, fatigue, and motivational constructs based on the Theory of Planned Behavior. The intervention (B) will consist of an mHealth intervention based on behavior change techniques, delivered via weekly calls, personalized messages, and a digital application. Data will be analyzed using piecewise regression models. In addition, a semi-structured interview will be conducted to assess patient experience. These qualitative data will help identify contextual factors, such as treatment-related side effects or variations in health status, that may have influenced behavior change and participation in data collection. CONCLUSION: This N-of-1 study explores intra-individual behavioral dynamics using intensive longitudinal data rather than testing a strictly reversible intervention effect. The mHealth intervention is based on behavior change techniques, with adjustments tailored to participants' progress and changing circumstances. The results will contribute to the evidence base for mHealth interventions designed to promote sustained physical activity and dietary intake among older adults with cancer. |
| A benign control-calibrated multiparametric functional assay supports CACNA1A variant assessment | Tan, C.-Y. | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.12.26365280v1.full.pdf) | CACNA1A-related disorders arise from altered function of the P/Q-type calcium channel Cav2.1 and encompass a broad spectrum of neurological phenotypes, including developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, familial hemiplegic migraine type 1 and episodic ataxia type 2. Although loss-of-function and gain-of-function mechanisms have both been implicated, interpretation of rare CACNA1A missense variants remains challenging because clinical presentation is heterogeneous and many variants identified through diagnostic sequencing remain classified as variants of uncertain significance. To generate clinically interpretable functional evidence, we developed a calibrated high-throughput automated patch-clamp assay to assess the CACNA1A variants. Twenty-six curated benign/likely benign and 30 curated pathogenic/likely pathogenic missense variants were expressed in Flp-In HEK293 cells with CACNB4 and CACNA2D1. We quantified current density, {Lambda}V0.5 activation,{Lambda}V0.5 inactivation and recovery from inactivation (T0.5 RFI), and used benign controls to define parameter-specific normal ranges with {+/-}3 Z-score thresholds. This multi-parameter framework classified all benign controls as functionally normal and 29 of 30 pathogenic controls as abnormal, yielding 100% specificity and 96.7% sensitivity. Current density identified 19 pathogenic controls with loss of function, while gating and recovery from inactivation identified additional abnormal variants with preserved or partially preserved current density. The assay generated likelihood ratios of 0.033 for normal function and 25.13 for abnormal function, supporting BS3 and PS3 functional evidence, respectively. Application to 23 CACNA1A variants identified in Australian patients showed abnormal function in all four variants already classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic. Among 19 variants of uncertain significance, 11 were functionally abnormal and nine were reclassified as likely pathogenic after integration of PS3 evidence with other ACMG/AMP criteria. These data show that CACNA1A missense variants can produce loss-of-function, gain-of-function or mixed effects, and that multi-parameter functional assessment is required for accurate interpretation. This calibrated automated patch-clamp framework provides clinically interpretable evidence for refining CACNA1A variant classification in diagnostic practice. |

Retained-dentition summaries may therefore underrepresent cumulative periodontal destruction when tooth loss is partly periodontal in origin. Objective. To develop and evaluate a framework for estimating periodontitis experience from cross-sectional data by incorporating informative tooth loss under etiological uncertainty. Methods. Data came from 10,324 dentate adults aged [&ge;]30 years in NHANES 2009-2014 who completed periodontal and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) assessments. Expected Periodontitis Experience (EPE) combined observed CAL among retained teeth with the expected periodontal contribution of missing teeth. Published extraction-cause studies informed age-specific weights for missing teeth. EPE was compared with mean CAL and extent CAL [&ge;]4 mm using survey-weighted age patterns and late-life curvature within glycemic groups. HbA1c was used as a theoretically related but distinct external measure. Results. Periodontal observability declined with age, particularly among adults with diabetes. Conventional observed-site measures showed weaker or declining fitted increases after age 70, whereas EPE retained a more consistently increasing age pattern. Late-life curvature was 0.06, 0.04, and -0.05 across glycemic groups for EPE, compared with -0.47, -0.91, and -2.70 for extent CAL [&ge;]4 mm. EPE also showed the strongest rank correlation with HbA1c ({rho} = 0.291 versus 0.238-0.255). Conclusions. Treating tooth loss as probabilistically informative may provide a more coherent representation of cumulative periodontal destruction in cross-sectional periodontal epidemiology, particularly at older ages. |

Despite the clinical significance of H. pylori infection, many genetic factors underlying host response to H. pylori remain unidentified. Moreover, although H. pylori infection has been implicated in a range of extra-gastric diseases, whether these associations share a common genetic architecture remains unclear. We performed a genome-wide association study (GWAS) of anti-H. pylori IgG antibody titers, a composite host phenotype reflecting susceptibility to persistent infection and subsequent immune activation, followed by gene-based and pathway analyses, involving up to 140,863 individuals. This included 56,967 individuals in the discovery phase and 68,211 in the replication phase from Japanese cohorts, together with an additional 15,685 from European populations in a cross-ancestry meta-analysis. Mendelian randomization (MR) analyses revealed directionally consistent associations between genetic liability to higher anti-H. pylori IgG levels and a range of diseases, including immune-mediated diseases such as type 1 diabetes, Hashimoto's disease, atopic dermatitis, as well as quantitative traits like body height and weight. These genetic findings support the interpretation that host genetic determinants of response to H. pylori, particularly those mediated through HLA class II, form part of a shared immunogenetic architecture associated with susceptibility to multiple immune-related diseases. |

Extracorporeal shock wave therapy (ESWT) is a non-invasive treatment that promotes angiogenesis and tissue regenera-tion. This review synthesizes the clinical evidence for ESWT in wound management, and asks how contemporary reviews reflect it. Methods: PubMed and Ovid/Embase were searched from inception to 1 September 2026 according to PRISMA, regardless of wound type, study design or ESWT modality. Fifty-one clinical studies and 46 prior re-views were analyzed. Heterogeneity confined the primary synthesis to a qualitative one; an exploratory pooled analysis was performed for the one endpoint reported con-sistently enough to permit it. Results: None of the 46 previously published reviews covered the original evidence in its full breadth: each cited a median of 19.4% of the clinical studies available at its reference date (range 0-90.9%), and systematic reviews covered no more than narrative ones. Most studies reported improved healing across all four modalities. In seven controlled trials reporting complete healing as events and denominators, the pooled risk ratio favored ESWT (1.51, 95% CI 1.22-1.87; I2 = 0%; 689 patients). Such data existed for three modalities only, resting on one trial for two, so modalities could not be compared. Conclusions: ESWT is a useful adjunct for acute and chronic wounds. Because no previous review reflected the evidence base as a whole, conclusions drawn from it should be read with caution. Head-to-head trials with matched physical dose are needed before any modality can be preferred. |

| A Growing Burden, a Persistent Gap: Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome Education and Emergency Department Trends in Michigans Legal Cannabis Market | Lorbeer, E. R. | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.09.26365113v1.full.pdf) | Background: Since Michigan's legalization of recreational adult-use cannabis in December 2018, health care providers in the city of Kalamazoo, Michigan, have informally observed an increase in cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS) presentations, although the extent of this increase has not been formally characterized locally. CHS is frequently underrecognized and misdiagnosed, often resulting in repeated emergency department (ED) visits and substantial diagnostic costs before a diagnosis is reached. Despite this clinical and financial burden, it is unclear whether consumers are adequately informed of this risk through point-of-sale or public health resources. Methods: A cross-sectional environmental scan was conducted to identify publicly available educational content about CHS on the websites of licensed cannabis retailers in Kalamazoo and relevant public health agencies. Provisioning centers and public health websites were screened using a structured coding rubric developed specifically for the environmental scan to assess the presence, depth, web page placement, and intended audience of CHS-related content. Additionally, the electronic medical records at a local teaching hospital in Kalamazoo were queried to identify ED visits with a primary diagnosis of CHS from January 1, 2010 to December 31, 2025. Results: Before the legalization of marijuana, the hospital recorded six ED encounters with the primary diagnosis of CHS between January 2010 and November 2018, and 274 ED encounters between December 2018 and December 2025 (mean age: 29 years; 157 females, 123 males). The incidence rate of CHS-related ED encounters was 57.2 times higher during the post-legalization period than during the pre-legalization period (95% CI, 26.0-157.3; exact Poisson rate-ratio test, p < 0.0001). Among the licensed Kalamazoo provisioning centers reviewed, only one mentioned CHS as a warning but did not describe symptoms or offer medical guidance. CHS content was not identified on the websites of the state or local health departments. Conclusion: Despite the growing clinical concern regarding CHS, consumer-facing educational resources remain scarce. Although this study cannot establish a causal relationship between the availability of CHS-related educational content and the observed increase in ED encounters following legalization, it identifies a substantial lack of CHS-specific warnings in the evaluated consumer-facing resources. Given the clinical and potential economic burden associated with delayed CHS recognition, we recommend that Michigans existing point-of-sale pamphlet requirement be amended to include CHS-specific warning language. |
| OSS-DBS v2: Simulation Platform for Biophysical Modelling of Deep Brain Stimulation | Zimmermann, J. | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.10.26365197v1.full.pdf) | Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is a well-established therapy for various neurological disorders. Clinical outcomes of DBS depend on electrode placement and stimulation programming, with growing evidence suggesting that targeting specific sites and networks can improve therapeutic efficacy. This targeting can be facilitated using high-accuracy, multi-modal biophysical models. In this article, we present the open-source platform OSS-DBS v2 that enables researchers and clinicians to build such biophys- ical models. Building upon previous work, the new version combines automated volume conductor generation, accurate and efficient electric field modelling and stimulation-driven neural simulations. OSS-DBS v2 provides an extensive library of intracranial electrodes, which can be modelled simul- taneously to test complex stimulation paradigms. Its application programming interface facilitates integration of external data workflows, such as impedance measurements. Furthermore, an integration with the Lead-DBS toolbox offered a straightforward graphical user interface for stimulation volume and pathway activation modelling, further complemented by an optimization framework. OSS-DBS v2 relies entirely on open-source software, supports all major operating systems and is designed for efficient execution on consumer-grade hardware. |
