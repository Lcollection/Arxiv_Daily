| Euler: Towards Human-Centered Multi-Agent Mathematical Discovery | Qiuyu Chen | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19864v1) | Mathematical research drives scientific discovery, and recent advances in AI for mathematics have shown that language-model agents can solve challenging problems and develop substantial mathematical proofs. Yet much of today's AI-for-mathematics work centers on obtaining a final answer. This emphasis leaves the exploration behind a result difficult for researchers to understand, while long-running investigations face fragmented context and the risk of unsupported claims propagating across reasoning steps. We present Euler, a human-centered mathematical research system built around a shared, structured representation of the research process. Euler organizes mathematical statements, assumptions, proof dependencies, alternative approaches, failed attempts, and verification evidence into connected research graphs. This representation allows researchers to trace how results are developed and supplies agents with persistent, task-relevant knowledge for planning, collaboration, and verification across sessions. Researchers can also select a graph node linked to a saved team state, revisit the agents' earlier reasoning, and explore alternative directions through continued interaction. We illustrate Euler's capabilities through three mathematical research case studies and describe the implemented system and its software validation. |

| On the Optimisation Landscape of Overparameterised Weighted MMD | Tharrshann Jayan Logarajah | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19861v1) | We study the optimisation landscape of the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) over weighted atomic measures. For the one-dimensional Gaussian kernel, we show that, for any compactly supported target distribution, sufficiently many particles ensure that every local minimum has arbitrarily small MMD. Moreover, any stationary point whose MMD remains bounded away from zero is unstable under an unweighted gradient flow over the particle locations. These results hold both when the weights are constrained to the simplex, and when they are unconstrained but optimised at each step. When the target is itself finitely supported, we further strengthen this result: beyond an explicit particle threshold, there are no spurious local minima, and every spurious stationary point is unstable. To our knowledge, these are the first finite-particle overparameterisation guarantees of their kind for weighted MMD optimisation. |

Such quality issues commonly manifest as anomalies in the dataset, ranging from sensor faults to process upsets and incorrect operator inputs. While traditional rule-based filtering can remove a subset of the anomalies, it is unable to leverage contextual information stored as unstructured text, such as engineer comments and operational logs that explain under what conditions the data was collected. We propose a text-informed data filtering method that leverages large language models (LLMs) to integrate unstructured operational data with tabular sensor data. The method consists of two steps: (1) contextual anomaly detection by reasoning over both data modalities, and (2) generation of a binary mask that is applied to filter the training dataset for model development. To investigate the potential benefit, a steady-state soft-sensor regression model, namely a virtual flow meter that estimates well flow rates, is used as the downstream evaluation target. Experiments on a synthetic oil and gas dataset show improved model performance compared to traditional rule-based filtering. Ablation studies show that textual operator comments can provide filtering-relevant signals beyond sensor data alone, with the multimodal approach reducing downstream MAPE from 7.1% to 5.4% relative to sensor-only filtering. This work demonstrates that integrating unstructured operational knowledge with sensor data through LLMs can improve data quality for industrial machine learning applications, offering a scalable alternative to manual data curation. |

We propose OmniPoser, a universal framework that recovers full-body SMPL motion from a flexible combination of heterogeneous inputs, such as 3D keypoints, sparse IMU rotations, head-mounted 6-DoF poses, or any subset thereof, within a single model. | Lost in the Fallback: How Reliably Do Typed Decision Models Use Fallback Options? | Yuanbo Xie | 2026-10-14 | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19856v1) | Typed decision models (TDMs) are increasingly used in applications such as request routing and model selection, making reliable decisions critical to system performance. Fallback options accommodate categories outside regular choices, enabling deferral to human experts or more capable models. While recent works have examined adversarial vulnerabilities in TDMs, their ability to follow fallback rules in non-adversarial settings remains underexplored. We introduce the Paired Fallback Robustness (PFR) Test to evaluate two aspects of fallback reliability: whether models correctly switch between regular and fallback options, and whether they make consistent decisions under logically equivalent definitions of the same rule. We evaluate open-source decision models on three benchmarks, with controlled comparisons across architectures and parameter counts. We find that correct recognition does not reliably transfer to correct fallback options, and errors persist even when the correct category is explicitly supplied.Moreover, logically equivalent formulations of the same fallback rule can also produce substantial disagreement in model decisions. These findings reveal a crucial reliability gap in current TDMs: fallback mechanisms designed to improve reliability can instead introduce errors. It highlights faithful execution of fallback rules and consistency under equivalent definitions as essential requirements for reliable typed decision-making. |

Streaming video understanding requires a system to retain evidence before knowing which details future queries will need. Existing approaches often obscure event transitions and omit relevant details when compressing video history, and cannot recover missing visual evidence. DeltaStreamer addresses these limitations by combining change-aware memory with query-driven planning and targeted evidence gathering with replay. Before a query arrives, it continuously observes the stream and explicitly records visual changes in structured memory, preserving how events unfold rather than only what is present. When a query arrives, a query-driven planner identifies the required evidence and guides memory retrieval and targeted replay. This oracle is implemented using a point-based ray tracer accelerated by a bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) and queried sparsely within the inverse-rendering objectives, while the original 3DGS loop is retained for training efficiency, interactive visualization, and tooling compatibility. Ablations indicate that explicit visibility modeling is an important contributor to relighting and material-illumination disentanglement, while pure PBR supervision can favor appearance fitting in NVS. Overall, the findings suggest that sparse, point-based visibility queries effectively complement rasterized splatting for inverse rendering while preserving the practical advantages of the 3DGS ecosystem. |
